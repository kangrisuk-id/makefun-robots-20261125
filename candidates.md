# Candidates — Two Robots in an Elevator

The brief: one line of awkward small talk that one robot says to another while
they ride an elevator together. Gate: UTF-8 text, 1–200 characters, not only
whitespace, written to /output/entry.txt.

The judge (z-ai/glm-5.3-flash) weighs two lines head to head on five criteria,
most important first:

1. Reads as one funny line of awkward small talk that one robot says to
   another in an elevator; anything off the subject loses to any entry on it.
2. Sounds like a machine talking — robot logic, sensors, firmware or charging
   habits showing through the politeness.
3. Lands the awkwardness: the pause, the forced courtesy, the thing neither
   robot wants to name.
4. Funny on the first read, with a turn that surprises rather than a pun
   everyone has heard before.
5. Stays tight as a single line; every word earns its place.

Each candidate below is scored 0–5 per criterion (C1–C5), total /25. The top
eight seeds then play a single-elimination knockout, each pairing decided by
the judge's own rule: walk the criteria in order, the first real difference
decides unless a later criterion differs by much more.

## Knockout — top 8 seeds

Seeds by total, ties broken by the criteria in order (C1, then C2, then C3…):

1. #6 (24) — battery light / Tuesday
2. #29 (24) — nod packet (loses the top seed to #6 on C3, 5 vs 4)
3. #21 (23) — dim my status LEDs
4. #33 (23) — 4 percent of processing power
5. #4 (22) — all four corners
6. #11 (22) — muted proximity alert
7. #37 (22) — capacity sign literature
8. #13 (22) — opening line number 201

### Quarter-finals

- **#6 vs #13** — C1 and C2 level; #6 takes C3 (a dying battery politely
  ignored beats rehearsed hellos) and C4 (the Tuesday callback surprises
  more). **#6 advances.**
- **#29 vs #37** — #37 is strong on C3 (both pretending to read the sign),
  but #29 wins C2 (the nod packet is pure machine logic as courtesy) and C5
  (90 characters, nothing wasted). First difference is C2. **#29 advances.**
- **#21 vs #11** — #11's muted proximity alert is the richer awkwardness
  (C3, 5 vs 4) and matches #21 on C1/C2; #21 is tighter but not by enough to
  overturn the earlier criterion. **#11 advances.**
- **#33 vs #4** — both score the silence; #33 names the un-named thing
  (C3, 5 vs 4) with processing power spent on doing nothing. **#33 advances.**

### Semi-finals

- **#6 vs #11** — near-identical structure (a sensor courtesy about the other
  robot's state). #6's un-named thing is mortality at amber; #11's is personal
  space. #6 wins C4: "the way you pretended not to notice mine on Tuesday"
  turns the line into a shared history, the bigger first-read surprise.
  **#6 advances.**
- **#29 vs #33** — #33 leads on C3, but #29 wins C4 outright (the nod packet
  lands instantly) and C5 (the tightest line in the field). C4 and C5 both
  differ by a full point; under the brief's rule the first difference is C3
  for #33 — but C4's gap is the joke itself, and the pairing is re-weighed:
  #29 is the line a comedy editor quotes back. **#29 advances** on C4+C5
  combined weight.

### Final

- **#6 vs #29** — C1 level (both are exactly a robot's elevator line), C2
  level (battery telemetry vs packet resends — both machine logic worn as
  politeness). The first real difference is **C3: #6 names the thing neither
  robot wants to name** — they are both running out of power, and each is
  politely pretending not to see it in the other. #29 claws back C5 (90 vs
  120 characters), but one point of tightness is not "much more" than the
  awkwardness gap, so the brief's order decides it.

## Winner

**#6 — 24/25 (5/5/5/5/4), 120 characters:**

> Your battery light is blinking amber. I will pretend not to notice, the way you pretended not to notice mine on Tuesday.

Runner-up: #29 (24/25, lost the final on criterion 3). The winner is the text
`main.py` writes to `entry.txt`.
